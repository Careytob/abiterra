# The True Cost of Buying a Home (Beyond the Asking Price)

Many first-time buyers focus on the asking price and are surprised by everything that comes with it. The exact charges differ by country and by property, but they usually fall into the categories below.

## One-off costs at purchase

- **Taxes and government charges.** Many places charge a transfer, stamp or registration tax. Ask a local professional what applies to you.
- **Legal fees.** A lawyer or conveyancer checks title, drafts contracts and registers the transfer.
- **Agent fees.** Find out who pays the agent, and how much, before you sign anything.
- **Survey or valuation.** An independent report can reveal problems and support your negotiation.
- **Financing costs.** If you borrow, expect arrangement fees, valuation fees and insurance.

## Moving-in costs

- Repairs or improvements you identified during inspection.
- Furniture, appliances and any fixtures that are not included.
- Moving, storage and utility connection charges.

## Ongoing costs

- Mortgage or loan repayments, if applicable.
- Property taxes and local levies.
- Insurance, maintenance and service or management charges.
- Utilities such as power, water and internet.

## A simple way to budget

1. List each category above and get a real quote or estimate for it.
2. Add a contingency for the unexpected. Many buyers hold back a cushion for early repairs.
3. Compare the total, not just the asking price, against what you can comfortably afford.

*This article is general information, not legal or financial advice. Rules and costs vary widely by location.*
