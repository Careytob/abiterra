# How to Inspect a Property Before You Buy: A Practical Checklist

A viewing is short, but it is your best chance to spot problems while you still have bargaining power. Use this checklist to stay systematic.

## Before the viewing

- **Read the listing closely.** Note the size, number of rooms and what is included in the price.
- **Ask for documents early.** Ownership papers, floor plans and any recent survey reports save time later.
- **Visit more than once if you can.** Go at different times of day to check noise, light, traffic and parking.

## Outside the property

- Look at the roof line, gutters and drainage for sagging, staining or blockages.
- Check walls and foundations for large cracks, damp patches or bulging.
- Walk the boundary. Make sure it matches the plan you were given.
- Look at the neighbourhood: access roads, nearby buildings and anything under construction.

## Inside the property

- **Water:** run taps, flush toilets and look under sinks for leaks or stains.
- **Electrics:** test sockets and switches, and look at the distribution board.
- **Walls and ceilings:** damp, mould or fresh paint covering a patch can signal an ongoing problem.
- **Windows and doors:** open and close each one; check they seal properly.
- **Utilities:** ask who supplies power, water and internet, and what typical bills look like.

## Questions to ask

1. Why is the owner selling, and how long has it been listed?
2. What is included in the sale (fixtures, appliances, furniture)?
3. Have there been any major repairs or disputes?
4. What recurring charges apply, such as service charges or local fees?

## When to bring in a professional

If you notice structural cracks, persistent damp, or anything about the legal title that is unclear, pay for an independent surveyor or lawyer before you pay a deposit. The cost is small compared with the cost of a bad purchase.
